## 1. An artificial intelligence-driven learning analytics method to examine the collaborative problem-solving process from the complex adaptive systems perspective
- Year 2023 | v18i1 art.2
- Authors: Fan Ouyang & Weiqi Xu & Mutlu Cukurova

**Haiku summary:** This study proposes a three-layered AI-driven analytical framework integrating learning analytics to examine collaborative problem-solving patterns from a complex adaptive systems perspective. Analyzing multimodal data from 24 graduate student group sessions using optimal matching, clustering, and hidden Markov modeling, researchers identified three distinct collaborative patterns: behavior-oriented (medium performance), communication-behavior-synergistic (high performance), and communication-oriented (low performance). Results demonstrate that effective collaboration requires synergistic coordination between communication and behavioral engagement.

**Ollama summary:** This study proposes a three-layered analytical framework integrating AI algorithms with learning analytics to examine multimodal data collected during groups' collaborative problem-solving (CPS) processes. The results reveal three types of collaborative patterns, associated with medium-level, high-level, and low-level performance, highlighting the complex adaptive systems perspective on CPS as an emergent, self-organizing system with dynamic and synergistic characteristics.

## 3. Learning to notice collaboration: examining the impact of professional development on mathematics teachers’ enhanced awareness in CSCL settings
- Year 2024 | v19i2 art.1
- Authors: Benzi Slakmon & Rotem Abdu

**Haiku summary:** Professional development grounded in Bakhtinian dialogic theory significantly enhanced mathematics teachers' ability to notice collaborative dialogue in computer-supported learning environments. Fourteen teachers analyzed video excerpts of student problem-solving before and after an 80-minute intervention focused on dialogic pedagogy. Post-intervention, teachers demonstrated substantially increased attending to and responding to dialogic attributes, particularly regarding relational dynamics, inter-animation, and multivoicedness. The findings suggest noticing dialogue is a learnable practice that can support teachers in fostering effective student collaboration.

**Ollama summary:** This study examines the impact of professional development on mathematics teachers' enhanced awareness in computer-supported collaborative learning settings. Through think-aloud interviews, 14 teachers' noticing practices were analyzed before and after a dialogue-focused intervention, revealing discernible improvements in their ability to notice specific dialogic attributes. The findings offer valuable insights into supporting collaboration and dialogue between students.

## 4. The perceptions of task cohesion in collaborative learning teams
- Year 2024 | v19i3 art.5
- Authors: Andrew Zamecnik & Georg Grossmann & Djazia Ladjal & Abelardo Pardo

**Haiku summary:** This study examined task cohesion development in collaborative learning teams using a mixed-methods approach combining surveys and temporal network motif analysis. Analyzing 194 teams across a three-week work-integrated learning program, researchers identified four perception transition groups based on cohesion alignment changes. Teams maintaining aligned perceptions demonstrated higher performance and triadic interaction patterns, while teams with divergent perceptions showed lower performance and dyadic patterns. Temporal network motifs proved effective as proxies for task cohesion, validating the integration of trace data with self-reported measures for understanding team dynamics.

**Ollama summary:** Researchers used temporal motif analysis to investigate changes in team members' cohesive perceptions and behaviors, including task cohesion, performance outcomes, engagement, and group synchrony. The study found that teams with strong task cohesion and high performance tend to share similar cohesive perceptions driven by interdependent interactions, while teams with different cohesion perceptions have lower interaction interdependence and poorer performance.

## 5. Talk to the machine: Unleashing the potential of AI to scale dialogic education and reduce polarization
- Year 2026 | v21i1 art.5
- Authors: Yifat Ben‑David Kolikant & Omri Hadar & Asaf Salman

**Haiku summary:** Researchers fine-tuned an LLM-based chatbot called LlamaLo on productive Reddit discussions to explore its potential for scaling dialogic education and reducing polarization. Analyzing ten conversations on contentious topics, they found LlamaLo effectively opened dialogic spaces by questioning assumptions and presenting alternatives. However, challenges including negative tone and bias emerged that could undermine safe dialogue and require computational and pedagogical solutions.

**Ollama summary:** Researchers fine-tuned a large language model-based chatbot, LlamaLo, to explore its potential in scaling dialogic education and reducing polarization. They analyzed ten discussions with LlamaLo on contentious topics, finding it effectively opened dialogic spaces by questioning assumptions, presenting alternative perspectives, and providing relevant knowledge, but also highlighting challenges such as negative tone and bias that need computational and pedagogical addressing.
